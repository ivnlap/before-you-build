# Example 02 — Scheduling app for private tutors → STOP on day 7

> Illustrative example. People and numbers are invented.

**The idea.** Private tutors juggle lessons, reschedules and payment reminders across messengers. Idea: a mobile app that books lessons, sends reminders and chases unpaid lessons, $8/month. The founder was ready to spend two months building it.

**What happened in seven days** (a `byb-sprint`, 5 h/week):

1. `byb-assumptions` ranked **A1 — "tutors lose money or significant time to scheduling and unpaid lessons every month"** as the first test: fatal, and the founder's only evidence was their own experience as a tutor.
2. **Desk scan** (day 2): many free and paid scheduling tools already exist; reviews complain about price and complexity, not about missing features (L2 `desk`).
3. **Experiment 01 — problem interviews** with 10 tutors (days 3–6): 2 of 10 described a recent, specific loss; 7 of 10 said a calendar plus a messenger works fine; 1 of 10 pays for any tool today.
4. `byb-decide` (day 7): **STOP**. The kill line (≤ 3 of 10) was hit on a fatal assumption, and no adjacent segment showed up in the conversations.

**The result:** about 9 hours spent instead of roughly two months of building. The founder kept two things: a list of 10 tutors willing to talk again, and an unplanned observation — 4 of 10 complained about finding *new students*, not scheduling — logged as a possible new idea, not as a pivot of this one.
